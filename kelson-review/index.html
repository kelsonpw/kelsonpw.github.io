01 / The philosophy

Every comment
has to earn its place.

The author’s attention is valuable. A finding should bring new evidence, explain a real consequence, and offer a useful next step.

01

Go deep. Leave less.

Read the surrounding code. Trace the affected behavior. Verify the concern before asking someone else to spend time on it. Zero comments is a perfectly good result.

02

Bring something new.

Read Bugbot, CI, human feedback, and the author’s replies first. If the issue is already covered, skip the repeat. Keep the review focused on what still matters.

03

Make approval easy.

Approve sound, scoped work. Let optional improvements stay optional. Use comments for substantial rework, and reserve requests for changes for demonstrated severe danger.

03 / What gets the attention

The behavior
people depend on.

Shaped by reviews across frontend, billing, analytics, infrastructure, and Wave. The skill follows the consequences of a change, then makes the smallest useful ask.

01

Can the customer finish the job?

Usable flows, accessible interactions, correct lifecycle behavior, and no new dead ends.

02

Are the boundaries intact?

The right organization, project, permissions, and region before anything gets written.

03

Does the evidence mean what it says?

Trustworthy events, preserved metric semantics, and clear limits on what has been verified.

04

Will it work through the transition?

Existing customers, plan changes, retries, concurrent requests, rollout compatibility, and recovery.

A few useful details.

Is this an automatic approval button?

No. It investigates the current revision and recommends a verdict. Sound changes can be approved with bounded fixes. Substantial necessary rework gets a comment review. Demonstrated severe danger warrants a request for changes. Required checks and merge rules still apply.

How is this different from another bug-finding pass?

It adds Kelson’s judgment about what is worth raising now. It reads existing feedback, checks the intended scope, and filters for verified, novel, consequential findings. A bug already reported by someone else does not need another inline comment.

What is the research behind it?

The skill draws on 502 sampled PRs across nine Amplitude repositories, covering review activity from January 2023 through October 2026. The corpus contains 912 submitted review events and 585 inline comments and replies. This is a bounded sample, not every review, a performance benchmark, or a claim of measured productivity gains. Current instructions take precedence over older habits, and explicitly AI-marked reviews are distinguished in the source material.

Does it post, merge, or deploy for me?

Invoking the skill produces a review recommendation. Posting requires your authorization. A review request does not authorize merging or deployment, and an approval does not establish production readiness.
